# Every Product Must Start With

**Pain:** The specific problem that creates urgency, frustration, risk, cost, or emotional need.

**Narrow ICP:** The first user group that matters most because the pain is strongest for them.

**Fast First-Value Moment:** The earliest moment where the user clearly understands why the product matters.

**Trust:** The user belief that the product is safe, honest, useful, and under their control.

**Habit:** A repeated usage pattern that makes the product part of the user's routine.

**Data Moat:** A defensible advantage created by unique, useful, hard-to-copy data.

**Platform Expansion:** The phase where the product grows from one strong wedge into a broader system, ecosystem, API, marketplace, or operating layer.

# Case Study Structure Principle

**Problem -> Target User -> Market Gap -> Solution -> Wedge -> MVP -> ICP -> UX -> AI/Data -> Tech Stack -> KPIs -> GTM -> Moat -> Business Impact -> Roadmap**

# One-Line Master Principle

**Build narrow, trust-first, habit-forming products that solve one painful problem, prove value through MVP and retention, target the right ICP, enter through a sharp wedge, grow through strong GTM/PLG/SLG, compound through flywheels/network effects/virality, defend through moats, and survive through disciplined funding, burn, and runway management.**

# Startup Terms: Clear Definitions

| **Term**                   | **Definition**                                                                                                                                             |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **MVP**                    | Minimum Viable Product; the smallest useful version of a product that proves whether users care enough to use it.                                          |
| **ICP**                    | Ideal Customer Profile; the exact first type of customer or user the product is designed to serve best.                                                    |
| **Wedge**                  | The focused entry point used to enter a market through one sharp problem before expanding.                                                                 |
| **Founder-Market Fit**     | The reason a founder has a strong advantage in understanding, building for, and winning a specific market.                                                 |
| **TAM**                    | Total Addressable Market; the total possible revenue opportunity if the product could serve the entire relevant market.                                    |
| **SAM**                    | Serviceable Available Market; the part of the TAM that the product can realistically serve based on geography, category, capabilities, and business model. |
| **SOM**                    | Serviceable Obtainable Market; the realistic portion of the SAM the company can capture in the near term.                                                  |
| **PMF**                    | Product-Market Fit; the point where the market clearly wants the product, shown by repeated use, retention, willingness to pay, and strong user pull.      |
| **GTM**                    | Go-To-Market; the strategy for reaching, acquiring, activating, and retaining customers.                                                                   |
| **PLG**                    | Product-Led Growth; a growth model where the product itself drives acquisition, activation, retention, and expansion.                                      |
| **SLG**                    | Sales-Led Growth; a growth model where sales teams drive customer acquisition, onboarding, contracts, and expansion.                                       |
| **Flywheel**               | A self-reinforcing growth loop where each successful action makes the next cycle stronger.                                                                 |
| **Network Effect**         | A compounding advantage where the product becomes more valuable as more users, customers, suppliers, or participants join.                                 |
| **Virality**               | User-driven spread where existing users bring in new users through sharing, invitation, visibility, or social behavior.                                    |
| **Viral Coefficient**      | A metric that estimates how many new users each existing user brings into the product.                                                                     |
| **Moat**                   | A durable defensibility advantage that makes the company difficult to copy, replace, or compete against.                                                   |
| **Data Moat**              | A moat created by unique data that improves product performance, personalization, accuracy, or decision quality over time.                                 |
| **Retention**              | The percentage of users or customers who continue using the product over time.                                                                             |
| **Churn**                  | The percentage of users or customers who stop using or paying for the product over time.                                                                   |
| **Activation**             | The point where a new user completes the key action that helps them experience the product's value.                                                        |
| **Time-to-First-Value**    | The time it takes for a new user to experience the first clear benefit from the product.                                                                   |
| **North Star Metric**      | The single most important metric that best represents the product's core delivered value.                                                                  |
| **KPI**                    | Key Performance Indicator; a measurable signal used to track progress toward a product, business, or operational goal.                                     |
| **Cohort**                 | A group of users defined by a shared start time, behavior, segment, or condition for performance analysis.                                                 |
| **CAC**                    | Customer Acquisition Cost; the total cost required to acquire one new customer.                                                                            |
| **LTV**                    | Lifetime Value; the total revenue or profit expected from a customer over the full relationship.                                                           |
| **CAC Payback Period**     | The time required to recover the cost of acquiring a customer through revenue or gross profit.                                                             |
| **Gross Margin**           | The percentage of revenue left after subtracting the direct cost of delivering the product or service.                                                     |
| **Pricing Power**          | The ability to increase prices without losing too many customers because the product delivers strong perceived value.                                      |
| **Burn Rate**              | The amount of cash a company spends per month to operate.                                                                                                  |
| **Runway**                 | The amount of time a company can continue operating before running out of cash at the current burn rate.                                                   |
| **SAFE**                   | Simple Agreement for Future Equity; an early-stage investment agreement that converts into equity later under defined conditions.                          |
| **Pre-Seed**               | The earliest funding stage used to validate founder insight, customer pain, MVP, and early traction.                                                       |
| **Seed**                   | An early funding stage used to build the product, validate the market, strengthen the team, and grow early traction.                                       |
| **Bridge Round**           | Funding raised between major rounds to extend runway, support growth, or reach the next financing milestone.                                               |
| **Series A**               | A funding round raised after evidence of repeatable growth, retention, revenue potential, and scalable go-to-market motion.                                |
| **Product Analytics**      | Measurement of user behavior inside the product to understand activation, engagement, retention, conversion, and friction.                                 |
| **Distribution**           | The channels and systems used to repeatedly reach and acquire users or customers.                                                                          |
| **Positioning**            | The clear mental category and value meaning the product owns in the customer's mind.                                                                       |
| **Segmentation**           | The process of dividing a broad market into smaller groups with similar needs, behaviors, or characteristics.                                              |
| **Smallest Viable Market** | The narrowest market segment where the product can create strong value and build early dominance.                                                          |
| **Product Roadmap**        | A strategic plan that organizes what the product will build, improve, validate, or expand over time.                                                       |
| **Business Model**         | The way a company creates, delivers, captures, and monetizes value.                                                                                        |
| **Unit Economics**         | The revenue, cost, and profit behavior of a single customer, transaction, or usage unit.                                                                   |
| **Scalability**            | The ability of a product, system, or business model to grow without costs, complexity, or failure increasing at the same rate.                             |
| **Defensibility**          | The ability to protect a company's advantage from competitors, substitutes, or copycats.                                                                   |
| **Product Strategy**       | The logic that connects the target user, problem, solution, differentiation, growth path, and business outcome.                                            |
| **Go-To-Market Motion**    | The repeatable method by which a company attracts, converts, closes, and retains customers.                                                                |
| **Market Pull**            | Demand signal from users or customers who actively seek, request, adopt, or pay for the product.                                                           |
